::: zh
最后一个误解，也是最有意思的一个：

> **即兴不是"没有准备"，而是"用已知素材与规则实时创作"。**

它需要准备的**比作曲更多** —— 因为**没有修改的机会**：
作曲可以反复改，即兴必须**一次做对**。

## 历史上的即兴是常规，不是特例

| 场合 | 即兴的内容 |
|---|---|
| **通奏低音** | 按数字**现场补出上方声部**（见 [[concept:figured-bass\|数字低音]]） |
| **装饰音** | 按惯例**自行添加**（见 [[concept:ornaments\|装饰音]]） |
| **华彩段** | 独奏者**现场炫技**（见 [[concept:concerto-form\|协奏曲形式]]） |
| **前奏曲 / 幻想曲** | 常带即兴性质（实例中巴赫的幻想曲即是） |

**换句话说：当时的乐谱是"骨架 + 提示"，演奏者要现场补完** ——
这与 [[concept:historical-performance|历史演奏法]] 的结论一致。

**"即兴已经失传"这个说法要修正**：19 世纪后古典音乐中即兴逐渐式微（因为记谱越来越写死），
但**爵士与大量民间音乐传统中，即兴一直是核心**。

## 即兴与作曲不是两件事

这是本条最重要的认识：

| | 作曲 | 即兴 |
|---|---|---|
| 素材 | 已有素材与风格惯例 | **相同** |
| 约束 | 和声、曲式、风格 | **相同** |
| 输出 | 写下来，可修改 | 直接演奏，不可修改 |

**两者都在"约束下做选择"** —— 差别只在**修改的机会与时间**。
**所以"会即兴"不等于"没有章法"**：恰恰相反，它要求对规则的掌握**更熟练**（因为来不及想）。

## 三个层次（也是练习路径）

| 层次 | 做什么 | 依赖什么 |
|---|---|---|
| **① 装饰** | 给已有旋律加装饰音 | 演奏法惯例（见 [[concept:ornaments\|装饰音]]） |
| **② 织体** | 按和声骨架现场组织声部 | **和声功能判断**（见 [[concept:chord-hearing\|和弦听辨]]） |
| **③ 旋律** | 在和声进行上创作旋律 | 调内听觉与素材组织（见 [[concept:interval-hearing\|音程听辨]]） |

**第①层最容易入门**：给一条熟悉的旋律**加装饰**，就是即兴的第一种形式。
**第②、③层才接近通常理解的"即兴"**，而它们的前提是**能实时判断和声走向**。

## 为什么值得学（即使以后不即兴）

| 收益 | 说明 |
|---|---|
| **理解作曲者的处境** | 知道"哪些地方当时是留给演奏者的" |
| **提高视奏** | 即兴训练的是**预判**能力（见 [[concept:sight-reading\|视奏]]） |
| **听觉更主动** | 即兴时的听觉是"找路"，而不是"跟随" |
| **音乐不只是执行** | 从"把谱面变成声音"变成"**在规则内做选择**" |

**第四行是根本**：它改变的是**你与音乐的关系** ——
从执行者变成选择者，而**"知道自己在做选择"正是历史演奏法那条的落点**。

## 听一听：同一个骨架的不同走法

即兴的实质是**在同一骨架（和声进行）上做出不同选择**。用 `progression` 听一段标准进行 ——
**同一串和声，可以有无数种旋律与织体写法**，这正是即兴的发挥空间。

## 常见误解

- **「即兴就是随便弹」** → 恰恰相反：**在约束下实时做选择**，而且**没有修改机会**，要求更熟练。
- **「即兴已经失传」** → 古典传统中确实式微，但**爵士与民间音乐中一直是核心**。
- **「即兴与作曲是两回事」** → **素材与约束完全相同**，差别只在**能否修改**。
- **「不打算即兴就不用学」** → 它训练的是**预判与主动听觉**，直接提升视奏与对作品的理解。

::: en
The last misconception is also the most interesting:

> **Improvisation is not "being unprepared" but "composing in real time from known material and rules".**

It requires **more** preparation than composing does — because **there is no chance to revise**: a composer can
change things repeatedly, an improviser must **get it right once**.

## Historically, improvisation was the norm, not the exception

| Occasion | What was improvised |
|---|---|
| **continuo** | supplying the upper parts live from the figures (see [[concept:figured-bass\|figured bass]]) |
| **ornaments** | added at will by convention (see [[concept:ornaments\|ornaments]]) |
| **cadenzas** | the soloist dazzling on the spot (see [[concept:concerto-form\|concerto form]]) |
| **preludes / fantasias** | often improvisatory in character (as in the Bach fantasia among the instances) |

**In other words: the score was "skeleton plus cue", and the player completed it live** — exactly the conclusion
reached under [[concept:historical-performance|historical performance]].

**"Improvisation has been lost" needs qualifying**: it declined in classical music after the nineteenth century (as
notation became ever more explicit), but **in jazz and many folk traditions it has always been central**.

## Improvisation and composing are not two things

The most important recognition here:

| | Composing | Improvising |
|---|---|---|
| Material | existing material and stylistic convention | **the same** |
| Constraints | harmony, form, style | **the same** |
| Output | written down, revisable | played directly, not revisable |

**Both are "making choices under constraints"** — the difference is only **the chance to revise and the time
available**. **So being able to improvise does not mean working without rules**; on the contrary, it demands a
**more fluent** command of them, since there is no time to think.

## Three levels (also a practice path)

| Level | Do this | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| **1 ornament** | add ornaments to a known melody | performance convention (see [[concept:ornaments\|ornaments]]) |
| **2 texture** | organise the voices live over a harmonic frame | **judging harmonic function** (see [[concept:chord-hearing\|chord hearing]]) |
| **3 melody** | invent melody over a progression | tonal hearing and shaping material (see [[concept:interval-hearing\|interval hearing]]) |

**Level 1 is the easiest entry**: **ornamenting a familiar melody** is the first form of improvisation.
**Levels 2 and 3 approach what is usually meant by improvising**, and both presuppose **judging harmonic direction
in real time**.

## Why it is worth learning (even if you never improvise)

| Gain | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **understanding the composer's situation** | knowing "which places were left to the player then" |
| **better sight-reading** | improvisation trains **prediction** (see [[concept:sight-reading\|sight-reading]]) |
| **more active listening** | the improviser's ear is **finding a way**, not following one |
| **music as more than execution** | from "turning the page into sound" to "**choosing within the rules**" |

**The fourth is fundamental**: it changes **your relationship to music** — from executor to chooser. And **knowing
that you are making a choice is exactly the landing point of historical performance.**

## Listen: many routes through one frame

Improvisation is **making different choices over the same frame**. Use `progression` on a standard progression —
**one set of chords admits countless melodies and textures**, which is exactly where improvisation has room.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Improvisation means playing anything."** The opposite: **choosing in real time under constraints**, with **no
  chance to revise**, which demands more fluency.
- **"Improvisation has been lost."** It declined in classical music, but **has always been central in jazz and folk
  traditions**.
- **"Improvisation and composing are separate."** The **material and constraints are identical**; only revisability
  differs.
- **"No need to learn it if I will not improvise."** It trains **prediction and active listening**, directly improving
  sight-reading and understanding of works.
