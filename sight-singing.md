视唱是"看谱直接唱出来"。它的价值不在表演，而在训练一件事：

> **它逼迫你在出声之前先"预听"。**

这与 [[concept:ear-training|练耳]] 的**第④层（看谱就知道大概声音）**是同一件事 ——
**视唱就是那一层的训练手段**。所以视唱不是"唱得好不好听"的问题，
而是**"你心里有没有先听到"**的问题。

## 两种唱名法

这是视唱最重要的一个选择，两者侧重不同：

| | **首调唱名法** | **固定唱名法** |
|---|---|---|
| do 在哪里 | **随主音移动**（调变，do 也变） | **永远是 C** |
| 强化什么 | **调内关系**（级数与功能） | 绝对音高感与视谱速度 |
| 与什么相通 | **与简谱思路一致**（见 [[concept:jianpu\|简谱]]） | 与五线谱的"位置"逻辑一致 |
| 常见于 | 民歌、声乐、流行、中国音乐教学 | 器乐、古典、音乐学院视唱体系 |
| 弱项 | 换调后绝对音高感弱 | 调内关系的直觉相对弱 |

**选择建议**：**两者都要会**，但可以有主次 ——

| 目的 | 优先 |
|---|---|
| 理解调内关系与和声功能 | **首调** |
| 器乐视谱、复杂变化音 | **固定** |

**关键认识**：这不是"哪个更正确"的问题，而是**两套映射方式**——
一个把"音级"当坐标，一个把"绝对音高"当坐标。**它们训练的是不同的能力。**

## 四步方法

| 步骤 | 做什么 | 为什么 |
|---|---|---|
| **① 看调号与拍号** | 先确定音高范围与时间框架 | 不看清就唱，等于盲唱 |
| **② 找主音与音阶** | 在心里建立这个调的"地图" | 一切音级关系以主音为参照 |
| **③ 标出难点** | 大跳、变化音、切分处 | 这些是**出错的具体位置** |
| **④ 打拍子唱** | 手打拍、口唱音 | 把时间维度与音高维度同时抓住 |

**第③步最实用**：视唱出错几乎总集中在**大跳与变化音**上 ——
**先把它们找出来单独唱两次**，再整句唱，成功率会大幅提高。

**第④步不能省**：不打拍子唱，节奏会随旋律的难易**自己变形** ——
难的音拖长、简单的音赶过去。**打拍子是把节奏"钉住"。**

## 为什么它对所有音乐人有用

| 用途 | 说明 |
|---|---|
| **练耳的核心手段** | 见上文：训练"预听" |
| **读谱速度** | 看得懂 ≠ 唱得出；唱得出的才算真读懂 |
| **内听觉** | 看谱时心里"听见"声音（作曲、编曲的基础） |
| **合唱 / 合奏** | 拿到谱子先自己唱一遍，就知道难在哪（见 [[concept:sight-reading\|视奏]]） |

**第三行值得强调**：**"心里能听见"是作曲与编曲的基本能力** ——
没有它，写作就变成了"写下音符、再弹出来听"的试错，而不是"写之前就知道大概是什么效果"。

## 常见误解

- **「视唱就是唱得准」** → 关键是**心里先听到**。唱准只是结果，预听才是能力。
- **「首调与固定只能选一个」** → 两者**训练不同能力**，理想是都会（多数人以一种为主、另一种为辅）。
- **「视唱与视奏是一回事」** → 视唱是**唱**（训练内听觉），视奏是**演奏**（训练读谱与反应），见 [[concept:sight-reading|视奏]]。
- **「视唱要练很久才有用」** → 见效相对快：**先把大跳与变化音标出来**这一条，就能立刻减少错误。

::: en
Sight-singing means singing straight from the page. Its value is not performance but training one thing:

> **It forces you to pre-hear before you make a sound.**

This is exactly layer 4 of [[concept:ear-training|ear training]] (**seeing the page and knowing the sound**):
**sight-singing is how that layer is trained.** So the question is not "do you sing beautifully" but "**did you
hear it inwardly first?**"

## The two solfège systems

This is the most important choice in sight-singing, and the two emphasise different things:

| | **Movable do** | **Fixed do** |
|---|---|---|
| Where do sits | **moves with the tonic** | **always C** |
| What it strengthens | **relations within the key** (degrees and function) | absolute pitch and reading speed |
| What it resembles | **the logic of numbered notation** (see [[concept:jianpu\|jianpu]]) | the "position" logic of the staff |
| Common in | folk, vocal, pop, Chinese music teaching | instrumental, classical, conservatory training |
| Weakness | weaker absolute pitch sense after transposition | weaker intuitive sense of degree relations |

**A recommendation**: **learn both**, with one as primary —

| Aim | Priority |
|---|---|
| understanding degree relations and harmonic function | **movable do** |
| instrumental reading, complex accidentals | **fixed do** |

**The key recognition**: this is not "which is correct" but **two mapping systems** — one uses the **degree** as
coordinate, the other the **absolute pitch**. **They train different abilities.**

## A four-step method

| Step | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1 read clef, key and metre** | establish the pitch range and time frame | singing without this is singing blind |
| **2 find the tonic and scale** | build the map of the key inwardly | every degree relation is measured from the tonic |
| **3 mark the difficulties** | leaps, accidentals, syncopations | these are **the exact places errors occur** |
| **4 conduct while singing** | beat time with the hand as you sing | holds the temporal and pitch dimensions at once |

**Step 3 is the most practical**: sight-singing errors cluster almost entirely on **leaps and accidentals** —
**sing those alone twice first**, then the whole phrase, and the success rate rises sharply.

**Step 4 cannot be skipped**: without conducting, the rhythm **deforms according to difficulty** — hard notes drag,
easy ones hurry. **Conducting nails the rhythm down.**

## Why it is useful to every musician

| Use | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **the core method of ear training** | see above: it trains pre-hearing |
| **reading speed** | understanding is not the same as singing; only what you can sing is truly read |
| **inner hearing** | hearing the sound inwardly while reading (the basis of composing and arranging) |
| **choirs and ensembles** | sing a new part to yourself and you know where it is hard (see [[concept:sight-reading\|sight-reading]]) |

**The third row deserves emphasis**: **inner hearing is a basic skill of composing and arranging** — without it,
writing becomes trial and error ("write the notes, play them, listen"), rather than knowing roughly how it will
sound before writing it.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Sight-singing means singing accurately."** The point is **hearing it inwardly first**. Accuracy is the result;
  pre-hearing is the ability.
- **"You must choose one solfège system."** They **train different abilities**; ideally learn both, with one
  primary.
- **"Sight-singing and sight-reading are the same."** One is **singing** (training inner hearing), the other
  **playing** (training reading and response) — see [[concept:sight-reading|sight-reading]].
- **"Sight-singing takes a long time to pay off."** It shows quickly: **marking the leaps and accidentals first**
  alone reduces errors immediately.
:::
